Orbit Arena by Inside the Cube (Minecraft 1.21.11, Fabric).

0.4.0: The Bridge, waves that keep coming, celebrations and a fly-round.
- The pause menu's button is now Orbit Arena (it was PvP Arena), and "Fighters (PvP)" on the setup screen is just Fighters.
- The setup screen has two tabs, Arena and The Bridge, each with its own settings in the same places, so nothing jumps about when you change what you play.
- The Bridge, a new game: two bases floating in space, joined by a bridge, with long grandstands (ten tiers) full of fans along both sides, on jagged red asteroids behind a wall of force field (you can't get to the crowd). Cross, knock the other team into space, climb their temple and drop into their goal; first to five. Teams of 1 v 1 up to 5 v 5, blue or red (you choose); bots play everyone else, your teammates too.
- Each base is an island with a temple on it, in two tiers. Your team waits in the room inside its door behind glass, which goes after five seconds. Ten steps up each side take you to the top, where the goal is: a glowing pit inside a box of force field that rises from a line of light round it and fades out upwards. You can walk (and hit, and shoot) through it, but not build into it. The deck's and the temple's edges are layered, a quartz lip over a band of light set back under it; the lights run along the edges, so a band reads as one continuous light. Blue's base is lit green, red's red. The bridge meets the deck cleanly at every width.
- Round the back of each temple, five monoliths float in a horseshoe, built the way LumenForge's Portal builder builds its monolith slabs: broad, rounded at the corners and the foot, a band above the foot, a capital with a quartz lip, pillars up each side with the face between them sunk, shaded from foot to top. Each has a crystal high over it and a beam of light up into the sky (green over blue's, light red over red's). Each goal against a team crumbles one of its monoliths into space and shatters its crystal. The score is top left: your team's banner (with your face), then theirs.
- The bots play both halves of the game: getting into your goal, and stopping you getting into theirs. In a team they all go to score while nobody's coming; when you cross, as many go back as there are of you on their half (the nearest first), always leaving one attacking, and any already on your base keep going for the goal. Attackers keep going for the goal (across the bridge, building their own way round anyone in the way, up the stairs nobody's guarding), only stopping to fight whoever's in their way or hitting them. Defenders meet you on the bridge, wall you off, knock you into space, get between you and the stairs and guard the goal. In a 1 v 1 the bot switches between the two. Difficulty 1 to 10: at 1 they walk, bridge slowly and sometimes walk off the edge; from 4 they bridge round you and wall you off; from 6 they play to knock you off; at 10 they're as fast as you, never slip and decide quickly.
- The bridge's rules are the bridge servers': a goal sends everyone back to their rooms. Kit: leather armour in your team's colour (or Netherite), a sword, a bow with one arrow that comes back three and a half seconds after you shoot it, a diamond pickaxe, two stacks of your team's wool and eight golden apples, given again whenever you come back. That's all you ever have: nothing can be picked up (blocks you break and arrows included). Build over the void, on the bridge and on the decks, but not on or round the temples, the monoliths or the stands (it tells you why when a block won't go down); you can only break blocks someone placed. Fall into space or go down and you're back in your room. No fall damage. The bridge can be 1, 2 or 3 blocks wide. Optional copper obstacles. Time limit: none, 5, 10 or 15 minutes (most goals wins). Spectator mode works: nothing counts while you watch.
- New blocks, in their own Orbit Arena creative tab: green, red and white light strips (a glowing white core with a line of colour along each edge: laid flat, a band of light with colour above and below), blue and red bridge wool (soft like wool, but a pickaxe takes it in a moment, as the servers' clay), and blue, red and pale force fields (fading out upwards).
- A new sky, in the stadium and on the bridge: the real night sky (NASA's Deep Star Maps, from the Gaia and Hipparcos catalogues: every star a sharp point, the Milky Way with its dust lanes, the Magellanic Clouds), and in front of it, close, a ringed giant like Saturn, east by north-east: pale gold and tan cloud belts with a storm or two, lit from one side, and its rings. You're in them: their plane runs just under the arena, so they sweep out from under your feet, across the planet's face and round behind it. The planet's shadow falls across the rings, and the rings' on the planet. All of it is made by the mod (no pictures), and it holds its colour under Tempered Light 2.10.3.
- Round the bridge: red rock spires at all heights, and rocks orbiting slowly round the map above and below it, tumbling as they go. All the rock is coloured in gradients the way LumenForge's Island Generator does its rock faces: pale red sandstone and granite at the top, through terracotta, to netherrack and dark nether brick underneath, in big patches and streaks running down, never in stripes. The islands under the bases go from pale grey under the deck to dark grey and into red at the bottom.
- The fly-round: before a match the camera swoops in from out in space, round the stadium (or the whole map from far out to the side, then round behind the other team's monoliths, back over the crowd and down the bridge to your base), letterboxed with the match's name, then 3, 2, 1. Ten seconds; Space skips it; it can be turned off on the setup screen. The camera now turns the way it's going (it could stay facing one way before), it fades to you at the end, and your hand (and sword) no longer shows while it flies.
- Mob waves: How many is now the number of waves (up to 50). Each wave is a little bigger, and the next comes out while the last few of the one before are still fighting, so it never stops. The bar shows the wave you're on.
- Winning: the result waits ten seconds while you celebrate. The crowd goes wild, fireworks go up, and the camera turns to show you celebrating from the front (arms up, the jump-and-land "Siuu", a fist pump, the aeroplane, a little dance). Press V any time in the arena to celebrate yourself; players near you see it.
- The crowd: three fans facing you hold up boards that spell INSID3 TH3 CUB3 (in the stadium and in each of the bridge's stands), GG signs are more common, and a few fans watch through spyglasses.
- Axiom works in the arena: switching to Creative (Axiom's editor) no longer switches you back. Mid-fight you can still be hurt and everything still comes for you, so the fight stays fair; outside a fight you can build as usual in Creative.
- Bosses no longer wreck the arena: the Wither doesn't break the blocks round it, the Ender Dragon doesn't break what it flies through, and explosions (Wither skulls) don't break blocks or start fires.
- The stadium is sealed: the tunnels are walled and roofed all the way along and end in a dark gate edged with light, and the asteroid's underside has no gaps, so you can't see out into the void through the build. Your arena and bridge rebuild themselves the first time you go in.

0.3.0: go again, a sharper floor, and fixes from the first real fights.
- After a match you choose: Go again (straight back onto the pedestal with the same settings), Change settings, or Leave arena. After a mob or boss fight the arena is put right first while you wait on the pedestal.
- Bigger hexagons, as in the reference: about 16 blocks across (were 9.5), drawn as clean one-block lines with crisp corners instead of blobs where lines meet. The pedestals now sit in the middle of a hexagon 28 blocks out (were 33). The band of light round the dais is edged with stone inside and out. Your arena rebuilds itself the first time you use it.
- Every kit has 16 ender pearls.
- Pearls, wind charges, shields and bows work while you're looking at the floor (right click). The arena's protection was stopping every right click on a block; now it only stops placing blocks and the like.
- You always fight in Survival: if anything switches you to Creative mid-fight (Axiom's editor, a command), you're switched back. In Creative mobs ignore you, which is why they couldn't hit you.
- Entering is more reliable: you're only sent in once the arena is built and nothing is repairing it, and if the game refuses the teleport you're tried again until you're in. Fixes being left behind in your own world, falling, with the match running.

0.2.1: a sharper sky and the arena button out of the way.
- The sky is now the 8K night sky (8192 by 4096, was 4096 by 2048), so stars stay sharp points instead of soft dots. Same colours and brightness as before. The jar is about 22 MB bigger and the sky takes about 128 MB more video memory.
- The arena button / Leave Arena is now at the top right of the pause menu, so it isn't covered by other mods' buttons at the top left (Lightreel's record and folder buttons).
- Checked: matches in the arena always play on Normal, even when your world is on Peaceful, so enemies stay and fight back (as 0.2.0).

0.2.0: something to fight, a crowd that comes alive, and a bigger arena like the reference.
- Enemies now appear on every difficulty: the arena always fights at Normal for its own enemies (on Peaceful the game was deleting them the moment they came out).
- The crowd is never still: every fan keeps choosing something to do (clapping, waving, both arms up and jumping, pumping a fist, pointing at you), more and bigger as the crowd gets going, and every head follows you round the floor. Sign holders pump their signs. Now and then a Mexican wave goes round the stands. Hands go to heads when you take a big hit.
- Crowd sounds (made for the mod): a murmur all the time, cheers for your hits and kills, a roar for "FIGHT!", an "ooh" when you take a big hit, an uproar with whistles and applause when you win, an "aww" and polite claps when you lose. They play on the Ambient/Environment volume.
- The arena, rebuilt (it rebuilds itself the first time you go in): bigger (the floor is 48 blocks out from the centre instead of 40); light grey stone instead of dark deepslate; thin, bright hexagon lines on dark grey; a pale rim round the floor; a bigger pale centre disc with a cross and ring drawn in it, a ring of light round the raised middle, and the middle stepped up three times to a hexagon of light; the pedestals are a 3x3 on a 5x5 on a black 7x7; three tiers of stands instead of two, with orange stripes and lights between them; tunnels framed in orange.

0.1.0: the first arena.
- PvP Arena on the pause menu opens the setup: who you fight (fighters, mob waves or a boss), difficulty 1 to 10, how many (up to 100; bosses up to 3), your kit, a time limit and how full the crowd is. Start fades you into the arena; your own things are put away and given back after (even if the game closes mid-fight).
- The arena: its own space dimension. A round stadium sunk into an asteroid, with a honeycomb floor of glowing lines, a lit centre dais, two pedestals, two decks of stands, an orange band with floodlights, four boxes, tunnels, and a roof ring edged with light strips, under a night sky with the Milky Way that turns once every 20 minutes. Floating rocks all round.
- Matches: 3, 2, 1, Fight!; a bar with how many are left and the time; enemies come out in waves until all have come. Win by beating them all; lose by going down (you don't die), running out of time, or leaving (Leave Arena on the pause menu). Results in chat.
- Fighters fight like players, better as the difficulty rises: strafing, shield blocks, jump crits, axe swaps against your shield, sprint resets, mace leaps, spears, golden apples; armour from leather (1) to netherite with Protection IV (10).
- Mob waves add tougher mobs as the difficulty rises (up to brutes, wither skeletons, evokers, breezes and ravagers), with more health and harder hits. Bosses: the Wither, the Warden, the Ender Dragon (experimental).
- The crowd fills the stands (on your screen only, so it doesn't load the server): astronauts, robots, critters and supporters, some with signs. They cheer your hits and kills, go wild when you win, and groan when you take a big hit.
- An invisible barrier keeps the fight on the floor and below the roof. The arena can't be broken or built on (except in creative), and it's rebuilt after mob and boss fights.

Sky: Deep Star Maps 2020, NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio (star data: ESA/Gaia/DPAC). The ringed planet is made in code by the mod. Earlier versions' sky: Night Sky HDRI 002 from ambientCG (CC0).
