# Retromon 0.9.0: Oak's Parcel, trainers who spot you, your rival in person, the opening (18 Nov 2026)

`Retromon-0.9.0-for-MC1.21.11.jar` replaces 0.8.1. The map jar is unchanged. Start a NEW GAME to play the errand from the start
(older saves already have the POKéDEX). Compiled in CI; not run here.

## The opening
- Before the title: the night sky, a shooting star streaks across with its chime and a trail of sparkles, lands with a burst, and
  INSID3 TH3 CUB3 presents fades in under it (as the handheld games' logo star). Then a white flash into the title, and Charizard roars.
  It plays once each time the game starts; any key or a click skips it.
- PRESS START: Charizard roars again as you go in.
- Oak and the others look straight out at you (not after the pointer). Oak talks with his hands: a hand out, both opened wide, the
  other hand, a hand to the chin, easing between them.
- New gestures: a clap, a ta-da (arms flung wide), a two-handed wave. The card you point at does one of eight; you say hello with a
  different one every couple of seconds; you wave goodbye by turns while the map's copied.

## The start
- Oak stops you the moment you step into the tall grass at the north of town (or past the tree line), checked every tick.
- You're put back only onto good ground (never the top of a fence), and taken to the lab's door: the door block nearest where the
  games had it, standing outside it, facing it.
- No POKé BALLs from the start (as in the games): POTIONS only. Oak gives you five with the POKéDEX.
- Your rival walks over to you after you name your first Pokémon ("!", his tune, the black bars), then the battle.

## Oak's Parcel (Red and Blue's first errand)
- Oak's back at his lab's door after your first battle.
- In Viridian, the Mart's clerk spots you ("!") and comes over: "Hey! You came from PALLET TOWN?" You get OAK'S PARCEL (KEY ITEMS).
  The Mart won't sell you anything until it's delivered.
- The old man north of Viridian won't let you by before the POKéDEX ("You can't go through here! This is private property!"); you're
  put back in Viridian.
- Talk to Oak with the parcel: your rival turns up, you each get a POKéDEX (jingles on the lines), then five POKé BALLs. The POKéDEX
  menu item works from then on.
- After that the old man's had his coffee, the Mart's open, and Oak rates your POKéDEX when you talk to him (the games' ratings).
- Route 22: your rival comes at you from the League's way. Pidgey L9 and his starter L8. Losing doesn't count against you (as in
  the games); either way he heads off after.

## Trainers
- Trainers pace a short beat (two or three steps each side of their spot, across the way they watch), facing their way again at each
  end and glancing aside now and then. The games' spinners turn about.
- They see you ahead of them, within about 70 degrees of the way they're facing, 5 to 10 blocks off (further for the ones who watched
  further in the games), or right beside them whichever way they face, with nothing in between.
- When they do: the "!", their tune, the black bars come in, the camera frames the two of you, you turn to face them, and they walk
  over by the path (round things, not just in a line).
- Anyone you talk to stops and turns to you, and goes back to what they were doing after.

## Battles
- You stand behind your Pokémon on the stage, and their trainer (or your rival) behind theirs. You throw your balls from your hand.
  When your Pokémon celebrates a win, you hop and cheer behind it.
- Fainting: it sags and keels over (its own faint animation where it has one), then its trainer calls it back as red light, or a
  wild one fades where it lies. No more puff of smoke.
- The stage goes up under the white of the wipe, so the shader doesn't show its shadow before the battle.
- The game's speed (TAB) applies to battles now: the steps, text, bars and animations all run 2, 3 or 4 times as fast.
- The clock stops during a battle by the game rule (your screen's clock and the shader's sky stop too, with no jumping back).

## Around the world
- Your Pokémon walks the path you walked, a step and a half behind, every tick: it turns your corners with you, eases to a stop and
  looks up at you. No more stop-start.
- Wild birds fly well above the grass; you can walk under them. They come down to land now and then.
- Lamps: one soft ring of light round each, instead of the brighter rings that showed as squares of light. The old rings are taken
  away the first time each place loads.
- The camera's a little lower (52 degrees, from 55). When you talk to someone, it only closes in as far as it can without going into
  a roof or a wall.
- People keep out of water when they stroll. Anyone standing on a roof or an arch (the Viridian nurse) or in water is moved to good
  ground nearby when the place loads.
- Your hero's cap shows in the world (the skin's outer layers are always on in a Retromon save, and the cap's on the head itself too).
- The games' signs are planted where the map doesn't have one within six blocks: two shelves stacked, facing the camera. SPACE reads them.

## Tools
- `tools/star_sfx.py` (the opening's chime). `tools/heroes.py` puts the cap and the sun hat on the base layer too.
