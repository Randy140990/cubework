# Retromon 0.66.0: rooms lit as the viewer lights them (John's 13:36)

## The rooms' light, the same in the viewer and the game

The Interior Viewer used to light each room as it drew it, with a sun and its shadows and the light from the sky and
the floor. The game drew the same blocks evenly lit. Now there is one set of light for both:

- **Where it comes from.** The viewer works out each room's light at every corner the game draws
  (tools/interior/shade.js). Each face is cut at the blocks and at the half blocks. For each corner it records:
  - how much sun reaches it, past everything in the room, with soft edges;
  - how open it is round about, so corners, wall bases and the ground under things are darker.
- **How it's coloured.** The colour comes from those two numbers and the time of day:
  - the sun comes in high over the open front, as the viewer has always had it;
  - the room's light comes from above and below, by which way a face faces;
  - lamps are off by day and on in the evening and at night;
  - screens and windows give off their own light.
- **The viewer.** The In game view draws only that light: no lights of its own, and no shadows from people.
- **The game.**
  - The light goes to the game as `retromon/interiors/light/<room>.bin` (179 rooms, 4 million corners).
  - client/RoomShade colours each piece's corners with the same sums.
  - The lamps are in those colours now, so RoomLightRenderer no longer lays them over.
  - The room is coloured again as the light changes through dawn, dusk and nightfall.
- **The shaders (Retromon Light 1.2.9).**
  - A room's own blocks (retromon:prop, block id 10090) are shown as they come, at up to 2.5 times their picture,
    as the viewer shows them.
  - Inside, everything else gets the picture's last touches as it's drawn.
  - Without the shaders, the colours can't go brighter than the picture.
- **Checked offline** (tools/interior/export/checkshade.py): every one of the 9.67 million corners the game draws
  across the 179 rooms has its light (exact, or within a 64th).

## Metal and glass

- **Glass.** It is see-through as the viewer draws it (the translucent layer), not cut out to its streaks.
- **Glass tables.** Every table's top is one clean picture with a soft sheen across a corner, and no stepped white
  lines.
- **Metal.** The Centre's and the Mart's blue posts, brushed steel bands and chrome trims show their whole picture on
  every face: a post's round sheen down it, a band's bright top edge and shaded foot.
